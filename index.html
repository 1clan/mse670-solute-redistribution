Where the equations come from

Solute is conserved in the closed volume: C₀ = f_s C_S + (1 − f_s) C_L. Local equilibrium at the solid–liquid interface gives C_S = k C_L. Substituting and solving for C_L gives the lever rule, and multiplying by k gives C_S shown above.

The equilibrium model assumes complete diffusion in both phases, so every part of the solid is at the same composition at any instant. It is the equilibrium reference limit — real castings often show coring because solid-state diffusion is too slow.

How to read it: for k < 1 the growing solid rejects solute into the liquid, so both curves rise as fs → 1. At fs = 0 the first solid has CS = kC0; at fs = 1 the solid reaches C0 and the last liquid reaches C0/k. The equilibrium model is an ideal limit and does not predict the terminal segregation singularity of the Scheil model.
